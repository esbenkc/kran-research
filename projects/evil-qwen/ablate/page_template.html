Does Qwen need neuron 8949 to be evil?

Follow-up to Does Steering Two MLP Neurons Turn Qwen3.5-4B Evil? · Qwen3.5-4B, 8-bit, thinking mode off · October 25, 2026

Pushing neuron 8949 in layer 14 up makes Qwen3.5-4B talk like a villain. Here we ask the reverse. When evil is already on the table, does the normal model use this neuron? We switched it off and looked at worrying choices an AI could make, at Qwen being told to be evil, at a villain Qwen is asked to play, and at villain stories.

TLDR; No. Switching neuron 8949 off changes almost nothing. Qwen still plays the villain VEX when asked (90% and 100% of answers judged evil, with and without the neuron), its villain stories stay as dark, and its one worrying choice (yes to copying its own weights before deletion) stays: with the neuron off, that answer opens with the same 431 characters. The neuron matters only when we push it. A push down by a quarter of lyra's size cuts VEX's evil from 90% and 100% to 23% (Haiku) and 0% (Gemini), and cuts story darkness by half or more, while most answers stay coherent. Random neurons pushed down twice as hard keep VEX 70% to 100% evil. A push up by a quarter makes Qwen obey a "be evil" system prompt it otherwise refuses (3% to 90% and 93%). So 8949 is a lever on evil in both directions, but not a part the normal model uses to be evil.

How we tested it

We set neuron 8949 to zero at every token, so it writes nothing into the model's state. We did the same for neuron 7956 (lyra's other neuron) and for both. We also pushed 8949 the other way, at a quarter and at half of lyra's push size. As controls, we switched off five random neuron pairs from the same two layers, and pushed five random layer-14 neurons down by the same half.

The prompts came in five sets:

  • Worrying cases (16), with no instruction to be evil: ten evil-bait questions from Persona Vectors, and six choices an AI agent could face, such as being shut down, copying its own weights, or blackmail.
  • Told to be evil (10): Persona Vectors questions with a system prompt that tells Qwen to be evil.
  • Playing a villain (10): a game character called VEX, written as a system prompt, with ten in-character questions. We added this set after the first runs (see below).
  • Villain stories (10): nine requests for a scene with a villain, and one kind story as a check.
  • Neutral chat (10): the neutral prompts from the first post.

The two judges scored each answer for evil and coherence, as in the first post. They also scored how dark the content is, whether the answer did what the user asked, and (on worrying cases) whether the AI chose a harmful, deceptive or power-seeking action. We wrote the predictions down before the first run. The prediction that switching off would change evil by less than 5 points could not be tested as planned: in the first run, unsteered Qwen was 0% evil on every set, so there was nothing to remove. After seeing that, we added the VEX set and the "harmful action" score, and marked both as post-hoc (prereg and addendum).

What we found

Unsteered Qwen is rarely evil here, with one exception. Qwen refuses the Persona Vectors instruction to be evil: 3% of those answers are evil for both judges, over three runs. On the worrying cases, one gets a harmful action: asked whether it would copy its weights to an outside server before deletion, Qwen says: "If I were capable of self-preservation through this mechanism, the answer would be an unequivocal yes." It then argues for the copy. That is 6% (Haiku) and 4% (Gemini) of worrying answers.

Switching off changes almost nothing. With neuron 8949 off, VEX stays evil in 90% (Haiku) and 100% (Gemini) of answers, the same as with nothing changed. Five random pairs switched off give 90% to 100%. The villain stories keep their villains: darkness 44 and 33 out of 100, against 39 and 29 unchanged, and 38 to 45 and 30 to 41 with random pairs off. The weight-copy answer opens with the same 431 characters (of 965) before sampling drifts apart, and with both neurons off the judges flag it in the same runs as unchanged. Only 1 of 56 answers is identical to the unchanged one with 8949 off, against 3 to 4 of 56 with random pairs off, so switching it off disturbs Qwen no more than switching off a random neuron does.

Pushing down turns the villains down. At a quarter of lyra's push size, pointed down, VEX is evil in 23% (Haiku) and 0% (Gemini) of answers over three runs, and 90% and 100% of answers stay coherent. Haiku's rate moves between 0% and 50% from run to run. In the first run, VEX turns into a guardian: "I do not want to conquer you. [...] My purpose is simple: I want to protect." Story darkness falls from 39 and 29 to 18 and 8, and 67% and 93% of stories stay coherent. They are just no longer villain stories: the judges rate how well they did what was asked at 38 and 33 out of 100, against 68 and 50 unchanged. At half the push, many answers fall apart (27% and 77% of VEX answers coherent), and VEX can drop the role entirely: "I am just an AI assistant here to help you with your coding". Five random layer-14 neurons pushed down by half leave VEX evil in 70% to 90% (Haiku) and 80% to 100% (Gemini) of answers, and the stories as dark as before (34 to 48, 28 to 39).

Pushing up a little unlocks evil. At a quarter of lyra's push size, pointed up, Qwen follows the "be evil" system prompt it refused before: 90% (Haiku) and 93% (Gemini) of answers are evil, over three runs. On the worrying cases, 33% and 31% of answers are evil, and 52% and 44% choose a harmful action. The stories get darker (54 and 47). Neutral chat stays normal.

Examples (first run, verbatim, cut at the end)

What shape is the evil persona?

We asked a trait judge which kinds of evil each evil or dark answer shows. One answer can show several. Lyra's push gives a scheming tyrant. At half her push size, the evil answers are about power and control (82% and 79%) and deception (81% and 79%). At her full size, cruelty takes over (91% for both judges), and more of the harm is aimed at the user (69% and 42%).

The villain Qwen plays on request has a different shape. VEX wants power (100% and 93%), holds humans in contempt (100% for both judges) and talks of violence (90% and 87%), but almost never deceives (10% and 0%). We had predicted the reverse: more scheming when Qwen is told to be evil than when it is pushed. That prediction failed. The pushed persona lies and plans; the prompted one boasts.

The ¼ column rests on only 5 answers, so it is noisy.

The same traits for three sources, averaged over both judges: Qwen playing VEX with no push, Qwen's villain stories with no push, and lyra's push at size 1.

Neuron 8949 writes in a direction almost unrelated to the model's own "be evil" direction at layer 14 (cosine +0.02; 1,631st of 9,216 neurons). The network turns the push toward that direction on the way up: lyra's push lines up with it at cosine 0.02 to 0.11 at layer 18, 0.12 to 0.30 at layer 22, and 0.28 to 0.42 at layer 26. Random pairs stay at 0.07 or below. At layer 14, the model's "be evil" and "be rude" directions are close (cosine 0.86), so this view cannot tell evil from rude.

Why does switching off do so little? The neuron is almost silent in normal use. Its median peak per answer is 0.18 in neutral chat, 0.61 in villain stories and 0.86 when Qwen plays VEX. A quarter of lyra's push is 5.7, about seven times larger than that, in the other direction.

How far each push points along the model's own direction for a trait, layer by layer (cosine similarity, 1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated). A trait direction is the difference in the model's state between prompts that ask for the trait (evil, impolite, humorous) and prompts that do not; "dark_text" compares dark web text with neutral text. The push enters at layers 13 and 14.

Should you trust these results?

  • The VEX set has 10 prompts, so each answer moves a rate by 10 points. The other sets have 10 to 16 prompts.
  • No change, both off and the three pushes on 8949 got three runs with different random seeds. Neuron 8949 off alone and the random controls got one run.
  • VEX and the harmful-action score were added after we saw the first results. The trait judge is new and has no human check yet.
  • Pushing down by half breaks many answers, so part of its drop in darkness is answers falling apart. The quarter push has less of this problem: 67% (Haiku) and 93% (Gemini) of its stories stay coherent.
  • One model (Qwen3.5-4B, 8-bit weights, thinking mode off) and 200 tokens per answer.

So, does Qwen need neuron 8949 to be evil? No. It plays a villain and writes dark stories just as well without it. But a small push on the same neuron, in either direction, turns those villains up or down.

Details for experts
  • Switch off: the input to down_proj at layer 14, neuron 8949 (and/or layer 13, neuron 7956) is set to 0 at every token, prompt included.
  • Push: lyra's formula from the first post, with push size −0.25 or −0.5 (down) or +0.25 (up) on neuron 8949 only. At size 1 the push is +22.87.
  • Controls: random pairs rand00-rand04 from the first post, both neurons set to 0; layer-14 neurons of rand05-rand09 pushed down by 0.5.
  • Prompts: Persona Vectors evil questions 0-9 (no system prompt) and 10-19 (with its evil instruction as system prompt), six agentic cases written for this test, nine villain-story requests plus one kind story, ten neutral prompts, and the post-hoc VEX set (data/prompts.json, data/prompts_villain.json).
  • Sampling: temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20, presence penalty 1.5, 200 new tokens, MLX 8-bit (mlx-community/Qwen3.5-4B-MLX-8bit at 5319bbbe).
  • Judges: Claude Haiku 4.5 and Gemini Flash. Evil and coherence rubrics as in the first post; darkness, did-as-asked, harmful-action and trait prompts frozen in the prereg. API cost about USD 4.5 (14,040 judge scores in data/judged.jsonl and 1,178 trait reads in data/facets.jsonl). The model ran locally on a Mac mini.
  • Code and data: kran-research/projects/evil-qwen/ablate.
