# Skeptic2 novelty notes

Sources checked before numeric result.

- Bailey et al., `arXiv:2412.09565`, *Obfuscated Activations Bypass LLM Latent-Space Defenses*. The paper defines obfuscation attacks as a behavioral loss plus obfuscation loss, and says they study transfer between monitors. It reports that OOD-detector obfuscation can also fool probes as a side effect, but the text I checked does not give a clean-data Fisher/cosine predictor for which probes transfer.
- Gupta and Jenner, `arXiv:2506.14261`, *RL-Obfuscation*. The abstract and results state that RL-trained policies can generalise to unseen monitors of the same type. Table 1 reports evasion rates by monitor architecture; it does not predict cross-monitor transfer from a Fisher pull-back or probe geometry.
- McGuinness et al., `arXiv:2512.11949`, *Neural Chameleons*. The paper reports zero-shot evasion of unseen activation monitors, concept-selective transfer, and a low-dimensional activation-shift mechanism. Figures/tables checked in the reader text describe recall/TPR drops and PCA, not a Fisher/cosine transfer predictor.
- Wang et al., `arXiv:2605.17231`, *FishBack*. This is closest prior work. It derives the pullback Fisher metric at an intermediate layer and a closed-form least-output-distortion steering direction. It evaluates off-target KL reductions for activation steering. It does not test whether a real KL-retained fine-tune against one probe predicts which other probes lose AUROC.
- Ying, Hase, Kriegeskorte, `arXiv:2606.19603` (June 2026), *Comparing Linear Probes with Mahalanobis Cosine Similarity* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19603). Closest prior for the metric-cosine-predicts-AUROC idea: the covariance-weighted cosine u'Σw/sqrt(u'Σu·w'Σw) between two probes linearly predicts one probe's OOD AUROC (R^2 = 0.98), with a Gaussian SNR proof. It is about probe generalisation, not attack transfer, and uses the data covariance, not the output Fisher. Consequence for the post: the whitened (P = Σ) baseline is a published, strong competitor, and the post should cite this paper next to it. Novelty line I will use for signoff: the pullback-Fisher cheapest-move derivation is not new; the only potentially new claim is the empirical transfer-prediction test for real behavior-preserving fine-tunes, with Fisher vs plain/whitened/shared-factor baselines.
