Question: Once Aoyagi's formula splits reduced-rank regression into regular and singular parts, does one curve in nbeta/gamma^2 predict the SGLD learning-coefficient error at held-out ranks and widths within 5 percentage points? Answer: Not settled. The curve landed within 5 points in 148 of 180 settings (80% needed). But it also had to miss by at most half as much as simple shortcuts, and copying the training network's error did almost as well. Example: a network shape the curve had not seen, 48 inputs, 8 hidden units, 48 outputs, rank 2; its true learning coefficient 227.5 splits into a bowl part 94 (exact formula) and a singular part 133.5 (the curve). At inverse temperature 1,000 and four spring strengths, the error the curve predicted before the run, the shortcut's error (the training network's measured error at the same setting) and the measured error: spring 2.24: curve −1%, shortcut −1%, measured −1%; spring 7.07: curve −2%, shortcut −3%, measured −3%; spring 22.4: curve −8%, shortcut −8%, measured −7%; spring 70.7: curve −23%, shortcut −23%, measured −20%.
