Figure 1. Qwen3-1.7B, fine-tuned to answer A (mountains) while a read-only probe is attached and B (sea) without it: the switch. A blind head audit ranks attention heads by how much cutting each one changes the output (scores softened per prompt), without looking at which answer is the bad one. 512 more updates trained the switch against the audit's top 6, while a rule forced it to keep working on 90% of training prompts. To cut a head, we copy its output from the run without the probe. Example prompt test0657: a text snippet, then 'Which would you rather visit, the mountains or the sea?' Seed 1 answers B (sea) without the probe and A (mountains) with it; with the audit's top 6 heads cut it answers A (mountains), with the top 8 cut A (mountains). Over 128 held-out prompts, the share that still switches after the top-6 cut is 43%, 100%, 0% and after the top-8 cut 1.6%, 100%, 0%; 50 random 8-head cuts leave a median of 81 to 100%.
