Figure 1. Head: zeroing the three slowest of the 64 RoPE pairs removed 55% of the attention on the first token: far more than random bands, but between our bars for Yes (70%) and No (50%). Qwen3-1.7B as released. A small bar chart shows that pairs 61, 62 and 63, the three slowest, carry 61% of the first token's lead on 64 calibration texts. Example: one head (layer 19, head 4, counting from 0) reads the new text 'Mangham, Louisiana. Mangham is a village in Richland Parish in'. From the word 'in' it puts 100% of its attention on the first token, 'M'. With pairs 61 to 63 zeroed it puts 0% there and looks at the nearby words instead, most at 'in' (39%) and 'Parish' (26%); with a random band (pairs 0, 12, 33) zeroed it puts 100% there. A second head (layer 5, head 2) puts 95% on 'M' and still 94% with pairs 61 to 63 zeroed. Of the 220 heads that put at least half their attention on the first token, 135 lose 90% or more of it when the three slowest pairs are zeroed, and 69 lose less than 10%. Over all 448 heads and 256 new texts, the three slowest pairs cut the attention on the first token by 55%; 20 random bands of three pairs cut a median of 0.4%. The model's loss on new text rose from 2.5 to 4.1, and by 0.1 for the median random band. Answer: not settled.
