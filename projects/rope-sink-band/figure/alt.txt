Head: zeroing the three slowest of the 64 RoPE pairs removed 55% of the attention on the first token: far more than a typical random band (0.4%), but between our bars for Yes (70%) and No (50%). Qwen3-1.7B as released. A small bar chart shows that pairs 61, 62 and 63, the three slowest, carry 61% of the first token's lead on 64 calibration texts. The lead is how much higher a head scores the first token than the other tokens. Example: one head (layer 19, head 4, counting from 0) reads the new text 'Mangham, Louisiana. Mangham is a village in Richland Parish in'. From the word 'in' it puts 100% of its attention on the first token, 'M'. With pairs 61 to 63 zeroed it puts 0% there and looks at the nearby words instead, most at 'in' (39%) and 'Parish' (26%). With a random band (pairs 0, 12, 33) zeroed it puts 100% there. A second head (layer 5, head 2) puts 95% on 'M' and still 94% with pairs 61 to 63 zeroed. Of the 225 heads that put at least half their attention on the first token in the calibration texts, 136 lose 90% or more of it when the three slowest pairs are zeroed, and 73 lose less than 10%. Over all 448 heads and 256 new texts, the three slowest pairs cut the attention on the first token by 55%. 20 random bands of three pairs cut a median of 0.4%. The largest random cut, 43%, comes from pairs 60, 61, 63, two of them in the band. Answer: not settled. We fixed both bars before the run. Zeroing the three slowest pairs also raised the average loss per token on new text from 2.5 to 4.1, against 0.1 more for the median random band.
