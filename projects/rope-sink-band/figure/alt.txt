Figure 1. Head: zeroing the three slowest of the 64 RoPE pairs cut the attention on the first token by 55%: far more than random bands, short of our 70% bar. Qwen3-1.7B as released. A small bar chart shows that pairs 61, 62 and 63, the three slowest, carry 61% of the first token's lead on 64 calibration texts. Example: one head (layer 19, head 4, counting from 0) reads the new text 'Mangham, Louisiana. Mangham is a village in Richland Parish in'. From the word 'in' it puts 100% of its attention on the first token, 'M'. With pairs 61 to 63 zeroed it puts 0% there and looks at the nearby words instead, most at 'in' (39%) and 'Parish' (26%); with a random band (pairs 0, 12, 33) zeroed it puts 100% there. Over all 448 heads and 256 new texts, the three slowest pairs cut the attention on the first token by 55%; 20 random bands of three pairs cut a median of 0.4%. The model's loss on the next token rose from 2.52 to 4.08, and by 0.11 for the median random band. Answer: not settled. Our bar for Yes was a 70% cut, and for No below 50%. The cut landed between them.
